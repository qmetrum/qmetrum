## [Your Firm] — Quarterly Portfolio Review
**Q1 2026 · prepared for [Client First Name] [Last Name]**

Dear [Client First Name],

Here's your quarterly check-in: how your portfolio held up, and the one thing I'm keeping an eye on for you. The short version — the quarter was steady, your mix is doing its job of smoothing the ride, and there's a slow shift under the surface, in how stocks and bonds are moving together, that I'd rather you hear about from me than from a headline.

### How the quarter went

Your portfolio is built as a classic 60/40 — roughly 60% U.S. stocks (S&P 500) and 40% U.S. investment-grade bonds. Over the past three months:

- Your blend returned **+2.3%**.
- Stocks alone returned +4.2%; bonds were about flat, at −0.6%.
- The ride was noticeably smoother than stocks alone: your portfolio's volatility ran about **9%**, versus ~14% for an all-stock portfolio, and your deepest dip along the way was about **−4.7%**, versus −6.8% for stocks.

Zooming out to the last twelve months tells the same story: your blend is up **+17.6%**, against +27.2% for stocks alone. You gave up some of the stock market's upside — that's the deal — for a materially calmer ride (about 12% volatility versus 19%). That trade is the entire point of a 60/40, and this year it worked the way it's meant to.

### The one thing I'm watching: how stocks and bonds move together

A 60/40 only protects you if the two halves don't fall at the same time. The classic assumption is that when stocks drop, bonds hold steady or rise — they zig when stocks zag. For most of the last two decades, that relationship has held.

Lately it's been drifting. Measured day to day, the correlation between your stocks and bonds is running about **+0.40 over the last 60 days, and +0.61 over the last 30** — where a 60/40 is designed around a relationship that's near zero or slightly negative. In plain terms: the two halves of your portfolio have been moving in the same direction more often than the design assumes.

I want to be precise about what this does and doesn't mean:

- **It hasn't hurt you this year.** Bonds still trailed stocks and dampened your volatility exactly as intended. This has not cost you money.
- **It is worth watching**, because a positive stock–bond correlation matters most in a sharp sell-off — the very moment the bond side is supposed to cushion is when a positive correlation lets it cushion least.

This is a measurement of what markets have actually done — not a forecast. I'm not predicting a downturn, and I'm not recommending we change anything today. I'm telling you what I see, so that if I do suggest an adjustment down the road, you'll already know why.

### What we're doing

For now, nothing that needs your action. Your allocation still fits your plan, and reacting to a drifting correlation on its own would be trading on noise. What I *am* doing is watching this monthly — and if the diversification your plan relies on keeps weakening, we'll talk about whether a small change to the mix, or a different kind of ballast, earns its place.

If anything in your life or plans has changed, or you'd simply like to walk through this together, just reply and we'll find time.
